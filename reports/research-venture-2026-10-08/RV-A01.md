## 1. Abstract
“Theosis” is retained as the requested report title. Here it denotes the architecture's proposed progression toward coherent, self-maintaining operation; it is not treated as a measured scientific property. The technical object of study is whether stable logical identity, explicit authority and durable evidence can coordinate independently replaceable runtime carriers.
## 2. Research question and hypothesis
Question: can the existing components support bounded self-recovery while preserving identity and durable context? Hypothesis H1: if ownership is transactionally fenced, snapshots are validated and carrier capabilities are observed, replacing a failed carrier can preserve logical identity without asserting preservation of volatile execution state. H1 is a development hypothesis. The local evidence qualifies constituent operations rather than proving the integrated hypothesis.
## 3. Architecture decomposition
Identity belongs to a versioned logical record. Authority belongs to an owner/worker/generation/lease binding. Execution belongs to Node, Python, native C, WASM or Chromium. Transport belongs to the selected physical link. Recovery belongs to a verified durable checkpoint plus runtime descriptor. Observation belongs to timestamped evidence. Separating these roles prevents an address declaration, a compiled artifact and an accepted external operation from being conflated.
## 4. State mechanics
Initial progression is STARTING to RECONNECTING to ACTIVE after worker readiness, client acknowledgment and first-frame verification. Missed heartbeats lead to SUSPECT and frozen input. A valid renewal may restore the previous state. Expiration permits one atomic claimant to increment generation and enter RECOVERING, then RESTORING. A verified restoration enters RECONNECTING. An authenticated fresh transport and client reconciliation permit ACTIVE. Failure is recorded as FAILED; intentional termination as CLOSED. Claims against terminal sessions are rejected.
Lease and generation checks protect database mutations. They do not reverse a web transaction already executed by an old browser. A receiver must validate generation and ownership at dispatch, and the external operation may still require application-specific idempotency or outcome reconciliation. The uncertainty is retained as evidence. Client clocks do not confer lease authority; the database supplies authoritative time.
## 5. Continuity boundaries and counterexamples
A stable KEX address persists while a UDP socket or WebRTC connection is replaced. A profile checkpoint can restore eligible browser storage and saved tabs; it does not reconstruct arbitrary JavaScript heap state. A shared-memory mapping connects processes on its supported host; it does not become a cross-host transport by naming it logically. A successful local norm calculation does not validate a multi-host mining cluster. These boundaries give concrete counterexamples against an overly broad continuity claim and define useful tests for H1.
The proposed complete continuity contract is identity continuity, durable-data continuity and explicit interruption/reconciliation of execution and transport. Recovery objectives must be measured from failure detection to first useful restored operation, with checkpoint age recorded as potential data loss exposure.

## 7. Research design for integrated qualification
Experiment A: contend two independent PostgreSQL clients for one expired session; observe winner, generation and stale-write rejection. Experiment B: crash a worker during an unacknowledged input; verify no automatic replay and document the unresolved external outcome. Experiment C: corrupt a checkpoint and prove recovery refuses activation. Experiment D: remove a carrier capability and verify logical identity survives with an explicit readiness transition. Experiment E: deploy an incompatible ABI to a single sector and verify rejection/rollback. Experiment F: lose telemetry and prove the system reports unknown rather than invented measurements.
Record distributions rather than only means: recovery duration, checkpoint age, frame latency, queue occupancy and repair attempts. Compare fault-free and fault-injected workloads with the same release and configuration. Do not infer one-way latency from unsynchronized clocks.
